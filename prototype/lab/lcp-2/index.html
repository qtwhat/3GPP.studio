Priority says who goes first.
PBR says for how many bytes.

The gNB grants uplink bytes to the phone as one lump, with no names on them. Logical Channel Prioritization is the rule the phone must follow to split that lump between its queues. It pays in two passes: first each channel's promised bytes, in rank order; then whatever is left, in rank order again.

Where the three numbers come from

The core network describes what each QoS flow needs. The gNB turns that into three numbers per logical channel and sends them by RRC. The phone only runs the procedure. The translation in the middle is the gNB's own design; the specification defines the knobs and the procedure, not the mapping.

Four things to keep

Three channels make six classes

Promised bytes of rank 1, 2, 3 come first. Extra bytes of rank 1, 2, 3 come after. A low-rank channel's promise beats a high-rank channel's extras. That is how one mechanism gives both urgency and a guaranteed rate.

Rank without a promise is rank among leftovers

A top-priority channel with PBR 0 has nothing in pass 1. When grants are tight, lower channels with a PBR are paid before it. This is why signalling bearers get PBR infinity.

The bucket is back pay

Promise that is not spent accumulates as Bj, up to PBR × BSD. A long BSD lets a channel catch up after a dip or a quiet spell. It also lets that channel crowd out lower ranks while it does.

LCP divides. The scheduler provides.

If the promises add up to more than the grants carry, pass 1 runs dry in rank order and the lowest promise is broken. The gNB has to size grants to cover them, which is what the Buffer Status Report is for.

What this lab simplifies. Bytes flow like liquid: there are no SDU boundaries, so Bj never goes below zero here (in the specification it can, because a whole SDU may be served once Bj is positive). No MAC or RLC headers, and no MAC control elements, which are placed ahead of data. All three channels are allowed on every grant; the selection rules of §5.4.3.1.2 (subcarrier spacing, PUSCH duration, configured-grant restrictions) are not modelled. One grant every 10 ms. Waiting time is time in the phone's queue only. The flow needs shown (rates, budgets) and their mapping to the three numbers are examples.

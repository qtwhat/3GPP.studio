The whole story, from zero

  1. The phone may only send when told

    A phone (the UE) keeps its upload data in queues. It cannot transmit when it likes. The base station (gNB) hands out grants: permission to send a certain number of bytes now.

  2. The gNB cannot see the queues

    To size a grant well, the gNB needs to know how much is waiting. That number lives inside the phone. Something has to carry it across.

  3. First, a raised hand

    With data waiting and no grant, the phone sends a Scheduling Request. It is a single signal that means I have something. It carries no size.

  4. The gNB answers with a small grant

    It does not know how much to give yet, so it gives a little. That is enough room for the phone to say more.

  5. The phone reports a bucket, not a number

    Inside that grant it sends a Buffer Status Report. Queues are bundled into logical channel groups, and for each group the report holds one index into a table of ranges, as in the ruler above.

  6. The gNB sizes the next grants from it

    The report describes one moment and starts aging at once. Fresh reports ride along with later data: when new urgent data arrives, on a periodic timer, or when a grant has room to spare.

Now you schedule. Blind.

You are the gNB. Every slot you decide how many bytes this UE may send. Grant too little and data waits. Grant too much and the UE stuffs the rest with padding. All you have is the last BSR, and the queue keeps moving after it was written.

Four things you just felt

Buckets, not bytes

A BSR field is an index into a table of ranges. The UE says which bucket, and the gNB has to pick a number inside it. Short BSR has 32 buckets, Long BSR has 256.

A snapshot that starts aging at once

The values describe what is left after the PDU carrying the report was built. Anything that arrives later is invisible until the next report. A report can be old and still right, or old and wrong.

Groups, not channels

The UE reports per logical channel group. Put the call and the backup in one group and the gNB gets one summed bucket, in the coarse 5-bit table, with no idea which part is urgent.

An SR is only a raised hand

When new data shows up and there is no grant to report it in, the UE sends a Scheduling Request. It carries no size at all. That is why the first grant after an SR is usually a small probe.

What this lab simplifies. One UE, two logical channels, at most two LCGs. Every PDU carries a BSR, as if the periodic timer ran each slot. The grant counts payload bytes only; headers and the BSR itself ride free. Data is taken from the call first, then the backup, which is a fixed order and not full LCP (how a real phone divides a grant is in the LCP lab). Format follows §5.4.5: Long when more than one LCG has data as the PDU is built, otherwise Short; values are the bytes left after assembly. Not modelled: timers, Padding, Truncated, Extended and Refined BSR, HARQ, SR timing, other UEs.

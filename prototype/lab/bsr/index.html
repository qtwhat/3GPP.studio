Buffer Status Reporting: what the gNB actually knows

The UE knows its queued bytes exactly. The gNB only gets a range per logical channel group, and that range is from an earlier moment. Step through five moments and watch the gap between the two.

    What to take away

    Aggregation. The report is per LCG, not per logical channel. When A and B share LCG 0, the gNB only sees their sum. Separate groups give separate fields. A Long BSR is used only when more than one LCG has data when the PDU is built, so configuring two groups alone does not force Long.

    Quantization. Each field is an index into a table of ranges. The ranges grow with size, roughly geometrically, so on the log ruler they look about the same width, while the absolute uncertainty for a large buffer is thousands of bytes. Long BSR uses an 8-bit index with finer steps than Short BSR's 5-bit index.

    Two different times. The format is chosen from groups that have data when the PDU is to be built. The field values are what remains after the PDU is assembled. A group can therefore appear in a Long BSR with index 0. Try Drain one group.

    Staleness. A report is a snapshot. New data changes the UE's queue right away, but the gNB's knowledge only changes when another BSR is received. Being out of date and being out of range are different things. A small change can stay inside the old range, so the report is out of date even though it still looks correct. Try Change within one range.

    SR, BSR and grants are three different things

    SR (scheduling request) asks for an uplink opportunity and carries no byte count. BSR is a MAC CE carrying quantized data volume per LCG. A grant gives transmission resources, and its size is the scheduler's decision. A BSR informs that decision but does not allocate anything.

    Triggers (TS 38.321 §5.4.5): a Regular BSR fires when data becomes available while all buffers of the relevant LCGs were empty, or for a logical channel with higher priority than any buffered one, or when retxBSR-Timer expires with data. Periodic BSR fires on periodicBSR-Timer expiry. Padding BSR fires when padding in an allocation is large enough. A pending Regular BSR can trigger an SR when no usable UL-SCH resource exists.

    Model scope and limits

    Ordinary NR, two logical channels and up to two LCGs, with no IAB and no additionalBS-TableAllowed. Byte inputs already count as BSR-accounted volume. The service slider is the payload left after control and headers, not the TBS. Timers, Padding, Truncated, Refined and Extended BSR, HARQ, real scheduler behaviour and SR timing are not simulated. Step 5 assumes a later reporting opportunity, such as a periodic BSR, with no further service. MAC CE sizes exclude the MAC subheader. A Short BSR with every group empty is drawn as LCG 0, index 0. Ranges use the exact TS tables rather than a formula.
